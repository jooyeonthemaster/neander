# Quote preview experiment

Open `/quote-preview` or `/en/quote-preview` with the existing development server. The route is excluded from indexing and is not linked from the main navigation.

## Three-step journey

The experiment and `/quote` share `QuoteStudio`'s sequence: **event details → experiences → receive estimate**. They open directly on event details; the start-method screen and event-template chooser have been removed. Existing saved configurations continue directly in this flow, and the clear action resets the quote back to event details.

Event details uses a free-text **event name** (`eventName`, at most 200 characters) instead of an event-type picker. Legacy `eventType` data remains readable for previously issued estimates. Operating days and **expected participants** use direct numeric inputs with numeric mobile keypads rather than −/+ steppers. Expected participants means the total across all event days, and changing it also updates an already selected perfume experience (20–5,000 guests, respecting the 300-person custom-box minimum). The perfume options can still refine that count separately.

Step two pairs the option editor with a live illustration of the automatically priced services: AI perfume, photo booths and kiosks. Space design, interactive media art and custom AI content remain visible as cards, but clicking one only shows a separate-inquiry notice and contact link. It does not add equipment, price or options to the quote. Existing drafts and incoming links cannot revive their former automatic charges. All three stages retain the quote summary. The final stage validates the same form and shows a local test completion in the lab; it never issues a PDF, creates an inquiry, writes estimate history or sends contact details. `/quote` retains actual estimate issuance. Its former bottom cost-and-operation guide has been removed.


Experience cards open a non-modal speech bubble attached to the card's name. A small tail points to the selected card, and the bubble opens above or below it according to available viewport space. The original live preview stays mounted on the page; there is no backdrop, duplicate preview, background inertness or body scroll lock. Compact numbered topics separate quantities, kiosk experiences, staffing, packaging, photo styles, branding and other options for the three automatically priced services. The footer always shows Previous, the current step, and Next with the next topic’s name; Finish options is offered only on the final topic after all currently available topics have been visited. Jumping to the last tab early offers Review remaining, returning to the first unvisited topic. Conditional topics such as analysis-kiosk quantities join that same sequence. Closing and reopening preserves the current topic and reviewed progress for mounted services. Placement is decided once, at the moment a card is opened: the bubble takes the height of its tallest topic (up to 640px and the available viewport, plus up to 32px of headroom when there is room, so a hint that grows by a line or two does not start scrolling), chooses above or below the card, and may align the document once to make that room. It first looks for a position that keeps both the card name and the whole preview column on screen; if the bubble does not fit there (laptop-height windows such as 1366×768 or 1280×700), it widens the search to positions that only keep the card name on screen and prefers the one that leaves the preview scene visible, so the bubble stays attached to its card with the tail pointing at the name instead of covering its own row. Only when less than 320px can be made available does it fall back to overlapping the card. While it stays open, nothing moves it — choosing options and changing topics never scroll the document, flip the bubble to the other side or change its height, so the title and topic tabs stay where they are. Content that grows after opening (for example the kiosk split rows) scrolls inside the bubble, and a fade with a small arrow at the bottom edge shows that more is below. Reopening a card, or resizing the window, decides the placement again; a resize never scrolls the document. If the page was still moving when the card was opened (a step change or momentum scrolling), the bubble re-aligns once after the page stops. When the visitor scrolls the page, the bubble follows its card on the same side, shrinking to no less than 320px before it overlaps the card. The original preview stays sticky. Compact pages fit ordinary laptop viewports; small windows, zoom and mobile retain the internal scroll fallback. The bubble's box has no CSS transition and its geometry is not a motion style, so it is never painted before it has been placed (the reduced-motion stylesheet turns every transition into a 0.01ms one, which still delays a value by a frame; the height measurement turns transitions off for the same reason). Escape, Close and Finish options dismiss the bubble and return focus to the card. Clicking the same card again, clicking outside or tabbing out also dismisses it. Because the bubble is rendered at the end of the document, Tab from its last button closes it and moves to the control right after the card (its remove button), and Shift+Tab from its first button closes it and returns to the card — focus never detours through the site header. While a bubble is open on small screens the page's fixed total bar is hidden: the bubble has its own total and Next, and two stacked Next buttons were easy to confuse. Site-select portals remain usable and consume their own first Escape. Changes update the original scene and total immediately. On small screens, a bounded compact preview prevents selecting an experience from shifting the cards.

## Connected options

- **AI perfume:** guests, 10ml/50ml, phone QR/analysis kiosks, total perfumer teams (1 or more; consultation above 3), custom scent and packaging. The former booth-size, build and area pages are removed; space production needs a separate inquiry and cannot add a hidden fee to the perfume quote. Displayed per-guest prices use the same effective discount tier as `perfumeGuestCost()`, including a short explanation when the next quantity tier is cheaper.
- **Space design:** inquiry only, with no online amount or configurable options. Previously saved space specifications remain readable by pure legacy-data helpers, but are excluded from the active quote editor and its illustration.
- **Photo booth:** ordinary or branded wrapping, illustrated before/after styles, print and phone delivery, quantity and custom capture-flow examples. Equipment uses the rectangular booth silhouette. One photo-styles topic contains eleven existing templates, browsed four at a time with previous/next arrows, keyboard or touch swipes. A separate full-width “Create a new style” control stays visible below the gallery pager on every page, with its own 0–10 quantity control. Custom production does not add a separate topic. Two existing selections are included, further selections cost KRW 200,000 each, and new custom styles cost KRW 500,000 each (VAT excluded), using the existing price catalog. Selected names persist in the draft, preview, quote summary and the shared PDF lines. Custom production retains its separate price calculation.
- **AI kiosk:** one gallery chooses the experiences for all selected devices (1–4). With one kiosk it is a single choice; with several it is a multi-select of up to as many experiences as there are kiosks, and the chosen experiences are split evenly across the devices. A row per chosen experience shows which kiosk numbers it runs on, with −/+ to move one device at a time; devices left over stay “decide later”. Each device still includes one existing experience, and different devices may use the same one. Numbered devices, screen illustrations and readable per-device labels stay aligned in the preview. Kiosks are rented for the event duration, with no monthly subscription selector or rental-month topic. Result printing is removed from the experiment; restored drafts also clear the former printer option and fee. Brand/content/dedicated development is a card selection in the same experience topic (step 2), below the gallery and split rows, with one shared development fee across all devices. No extra tab hides these choices; a compact group shows the default, branding, content customization and dedicated development prices together. Korean uses one desktop row or two mobile rows; English labels and prices are longer, so English always uses two rows of two. Cards have a minimum height instead of a fixed one, every card matches the tallest, and the selection mark has its own reserved corner so it never overlaps the label. The experiment no longer offers multiple experiences on a single device or charges extra-content fees for assigning experiences to separate kiosks.
- **Interactive media art:** inquiry only, with no online amount, sensor pricing or configurable options.
- **Custom AI:** inquiry only, with no online scope, planning, payment-integration or maintenance prices.
- Shared event conditions and production `calculateQuote()` keep VAT and warnings consistent. If an older or external input still contains an inquiry-only service, the engine returns an unpriced (`tbd`) line and excludes it from totals and from transport, staffing, setup and extended-hour fee eligibility. New PDF issuance rejects an inquiry-only selection with HTTP 400 (`consultation_required`); previously signed estimate links still use their original amount snapshots.

## Prices, totals and small-screen controls (2026-10-02 review)

- **One VAT rule.** Prices of things a visitor adds (options, experiences, per-experience amounts in the summary) are before VAT and say so; totals include VAT and show the before-VAT figure beside them. The bubble footer shows the event total with “(별도 …)”; the summary labels its subtotal “부가세 별도” and notes that per-experience amounts exclude VAT.
- **Change indicator.** When the total changes, a small chip (“+2,200,000원”) appears next to it for about two seconds in the summary, the bubble footer and the mobile bar. It is decorative (`aria-hidden`), floats over the bubble footer's border so nothing reflows, and under reduced motion it appears and disappears without movement.
- **Touch targets.** On touch screens and windows narrower than 640px (the `touch:` variant in `globals.css`), the bubble's close button, topic tabs, gallery arrows, split −/+ buttons, remove button and Back/Next/Finish buttons are at least 44×44px. The demo link keeps its visible size and gets a larger hit area. A card's remove button has a 44px box with the same 32px visual, surrounded by an inert band so a near miss does not open the card.
- **Contrast.** Small informational text in the summary, price breakdown, topic progress and unvisited topic tabs uses slate-500/600, and white-on-teal buttons use teal-700, for at least 4.5:1.
- **Estimate form.** Submitting with empty fields focuses the first one that needs attention and explains each field in place (name, email, consent) while keeping the single summary alert. Once the estimate has been received, the summary panel's “견적서 받기” button is removed. The summary button on event details is a plain Next with an arrow; on experiences it continues to the estimate form.

## Native SVG artwork

The experiment uses native SVG shapes throughout: scene, product, service cards, templates, selected-service summaries and experience suggestions. No photographic layers are loaded by the lab. Older raster assets remain archived for reference and are not used in this renderer.

`illustrations/VectorPrimitives.tsx` defines a common palette, stroke treatment and ground-anchored equipment, people, counter and product components. Warm ivory, muted sage, teal and restrained coral match the site. The kiosk silhouette follows the existing OKPOS event references: a narrow portrait terminal, separate plinth, metal receipt band and external camera.

`EventPreview` defaults to `EventScene`, with a single shared floor and a pure `event-scene` inventory/layout resolver. The active quote editor supplies only perfume, photo-booth and kiosk items: every selected photo booth, analysis kiosk, rental kiosk and perfumer team remains represented, and rental kiosks retain their assigned experience icons. Different stations cannot occlude each other. Active-service highlighting does not change object positions. The compact legend switches the option editor without removing the remaining services.

The underlying SVG resolvers and archived illustrations still support older space, media-art and custom-AI configurations for reuse and legacy-data inspection. Those capabilities do not expose their former controls, equipment or prices in the current online estimator. The drawing is a configuration illustration, not an installation-capacity guarantee.

Brown perfume racks use `ceil(perfume.options.guests / 50)` in both the event overview and perfume detail: 50 guests = 1 rack, 100 = 2, 200 = 4. Each rack repeats the original bottle artwork without stretching it. The supporting counter widens; above six racks, parallel tables distribute the full quantity. Rack counts follow perfume participants, independently of general event attendance, days and the selected perfumer teams. This affects the illustrative furniture only and introduces no additional quote line.

Perfumer staffing uses 40 guests per team per hour: `ceil(perfumeGuests / (40 × hoursPerDay × days))`, with at least one team and no business ceiling. The quote model automatically enforces this minimum when guests or the schedule change, including saved drafts and PDF/API input. `extraTeams` remains the effective quantity beyond the included team; optional `requestedExtraTeams` remembers the customer's preference only while automatic staffing raises it. Fewer guests or more operating hours release automatic teams while retaining manually selected teams. Regional team travel fees and illustrated headcount follow the same effective quantity. More than three teams triggers a non-blocking consultation notice in the editor and quote, whether selected manually or automatically. Every selected team is retained and billed at the current regional daily rate; staffing, operations and final pricing are confirmed in consultation. The notice disappears when the effective team count returns to three or fewer.

An empty selection shows only that scene's empty floor, with no example equipment or people. Removing the last experience restores the same empty scene.

All primary scene views share `PreviewCanvas`: its responsive viewport height is independent of selection, equipment count and SVG aspect ratio. Each scene fits within that frame. Zoom buttons (50–300%), pointer dragging, touch pinching and double-click zoom let visitors inspect a particular area; “Fit all” restores the centered view. Keyboard users can focus the scene and use +/−, arrows and 0/Home. Wheel input over the scene zooms around the pointer without scrolling the page; scrolling outside the scene works normally. Pixel, line and page wheel deltas are normalized, and Ctrl/Command + wheel (including trackpad pinch) remains supported. Camera state stays local to the preview, persists while options change in the same scene, and never changes quote input, prices or saved drafts. Transitions respect reduced-motion preferences.

Canvas controls and gestures leave the nonmodal options balloon open so visitors can alternate between configuring and inspecting. `tests/e2e/preview-canvas.spec.ts` covers fixed dimensions from empty to maximum inventory, zoom limits, anchored zoom, dragging, real touch pinching, reset geometry, unchanged quote data and automatically priced service detail views on desktop and mobile. Camera math has separate unit coverage, including combined pinch zoom/translation at a pan boundary.

Clipping belongs only to the fixed viewport. Primary SVG roots inside the moving stage allow overflow so the existing floor beyond the initial fitted view stays visible while panning. Their original SVG rectangles must not become a second clipping boundary. Inner card and thumbnail clipping remains unchanged.

The shared venue uses a consistent 2:1 oblique depth direction, matching the equipment's visible sides. Camera bounds include the visible equipment and labels. “Experience details” replaces the combined scene with the existing detailed renderer for the active perfume, photo-booth or kiosk service. Bottle/packaging, photo template previews, per-device experience names and explanatory content remain available there without stacking them beneath the default event scene. Returning to a different service opens the shared overview. Archived space and custom-content renderers remain available in the source, but inquiry-only cards do not activate them in the estimator.

The perfume scene uses an 800×520 coordinate system and a common oblique projection. A single counter, the selected 0–4 kiosks and distinct two-person teams respond to options. Team selection starts at one, with no business ceiling. The existing `extraTeams` field stores the quantity beyond the included team; normalization and direct calculation retain larger selections. Three teams is a consultation threshold, not an input limit. Each team contains one perfumer and one assistant; general event staff are not counted as perfumers. Teams beyond the staged area appear below as distinct illustrations, with their count and workspace explanation. Both scenes draw up to twelve representative teams to keep very large selections responsive, and explicitly show the actual total when it exceeds that illustration budget. Quote quantities and fees always use the full count. Selecting more staff or equipment never silently enlarges paid floor area or adds tables. This is a configuration illustration, not a scale installation drawing.

The takeaway preview responds to bottle volume and packaging. Changing perfume equipment, staffing or packaging options returns attention to the combined scene. Short opacity transitions respect reduced-motion preferences.

Nineteen reusable standalone SVG exports and a manifest are in `public/images/quote-preview/vector/`. Rebuild them with:

```bash
npx tsx scripts/export-quote-illustrations.tsx
```

See [the artwork guide](quote-illustration-assets.md) for component anchors, the palette and the export inventory. The interactive page uses the same source components directly, so shapes remain editable and sharp at every screen size.

## Isolation and reuse

`QuoteStudio` accepts optional `renderExperienceEditor` and `renderArtwork` callbacks plus a `mode` that defaults to `live`. `QuoteOptions` also accepts an optional `renderExperienceIllustration` callback, `enablePhotoBoothTemplates` and `enableKioskAssignments` (both default false), and `allowKioskSubscription` (default true). Both `/quote` and the lab use `VisualQuoteStudio` with the same illustrated editor, photo-template picker and per-device kiosk assignments; their `mode` controls real issuance versus local-only completion. The visual editor sets `allowKioskSubscription` to false.

For photo booths, an explicit `templateSlugs` array is allowlisted and deduplicated; extra-style quantity is derived from the selected count beyond two. Old drafts without this field retain their previous quantity and price. The template catalog imports no demo UI or generation code. Merely choosing a template does not launch a demo or generate an image.

“데모 체험” / “Try demo” opens the existing service `DemoModal` on the current page. While it is open, the option bubble is suspended and its outside-click/Escape handling pauses. Closing the demo or choosing its return action restores the same topic, carousel page, selections and focused demo button. The embedded demo replaces service/quote navigation with a return action and retries module loading without reloading the document. No new tab is created. The normal service-page demo keeps its existing navigation.

The photo-booth option bubble has four topics: essentials → photo styles → prints and booth wrapping → capture screens. Extra print rolls and branded booth wrapping share the third topic, with their original independent prices and saved values.

The perfume option bubble begins with essentials, followed by analysis and optional analysis-kiosk quantity, then scent and packaging. It has no space or area topics. Essentials places the total perfumer team count directly below the guest count, alongside bottle size; there is no separate team topic. Every team is charged the regional daily travel fee, including the first, so the team price reads “each team … /day” (Korean: “팀당 … /일 (기본 1팀부터 적용)”), and a one-line breakdown under the controls shows guest cost + team travel (+ any minimum-charge adjustment) = subtotal before VAT. `perfumeBaseBreakdown()` only presents these amounts; `tests/unit/perfume-breakdown.test.ts` keeps it equal to the `calculateQuote()` lines. The final scent/packaging topic offers the included 30-scents configuration (default) or event-exclusive scent development for KRW890,000 before VAT, charged once independently of guests, teams and event days. This price uses the shared catalog, including the production estimator. Existing saved scent selections are retained. Three packaging choices stay in one row at mobile widths. The custom-box minimum of 300 guests, the price-change notice and original guest-count restoration within the mounted editor remain unchanged; redundant minimum-quantity copy is omitted while that notice is visible.

Kiosk experience selection uses illustrated thumbnail cards, four per gallery page, matching the photo-style gallery. The existing 24 experiences remain available in six horizontal pages. Arrow buttons, swipe and keyboard gallery navigation browse without changing the estimate. With one kiosk the cards are radios; with several they are checkboxes (cards beyond the limit are dimmed until one is removed) and the carousel is named “키오스크에 넣을 체험 둘러보기 · 여러 개 선택” / “Browse experiences for your kiosks, choose one or more” — it no longer claims to be kiosk 1's selector. Each card opens its demo in the same-tab modal and returns to the same option step. A persistent selection label and “Decide later” action keep off-page selections clear. The quote summary groups devices by experience; when nothing has been chosen it reads “3대 · 체험 미정” rather than repeating the count.

For kiosks, `experienceSlugs` is a positional array with exactly one slot per active device. Blank slots are undecided; duplicate slugs are allowed. Increasing the count adds blank slots; decreasing it removes trailing slots. A missing array migrates the old single choice to the first device only. Explicitly cleared or malformed arrays never resurrect the old choice. The allowlist matches the existing 24 kiosk experiences. `normalizePreviewInput()` applies this contract to loaded drafts, incoming service links and edits; a service link updates the first device while retaining other assignments. The server sanitizes the new field and uses the same per-device details in quote/PDF lines. Old monthly kiosk selections in restored drafts and service links are normalized to event rental (`mode: event`, `months: 1`), retaining device counts, assignments and customization while clearing the removed printer option. Totals use event days and event operating conditions. The service illustration follows the same normalization. Previously issued live estimates retain their original signed options and price snapshots. The shared monthly calculation helper remains for legacy data, but is not a selectable mode in the illustrated quote flow.

`ServicePreview` receives `{ input, lang, activeKey }` and chooses the matching detail illustration. Perfume and space delegate to `QuotePreview`. `EventPreview` owns only the overview/detail viewing state; neither renderer nor the pure scene resolvers change prices or quote input. The combined view is a configuration illustration, not a confirmed installation plan.

Both illustrated routes use `QuoteOptions` with its `paged` layout, without a perfume space `leadingPage`. Selected editors and hidden topic panels remain mounted while switching services or closing the options window within the experiences stage to preserve custom-box guest-count restoration. Options persist across all three stages. Contact fields are never stored in the draft. Production quote analytics are skipped in preview mode.

Browser persistence uses `neander-quote-preview-flow-v1`. If no flow draft exists, the earlier lab's `neander-quote-preview-v1` selection is imported once. Clear saves an empty flow draft so that an old selection cannot reappear. Production drafts and issued estimates are never read or written.

## Verification

```bash
npm test
npx playwright test tests/e2e/quote-simplified-flow.spec.ts
npx eslint src/components/quote-preview src/lib/quote-preview 'src/app/[locale]/quote-preview/page.tsx' scripts/export-quote-illustrations.tsx
npx tsc --noEmit
```

Current browser checks cover desktop/mobile three-stage navigation, event-name and numeric input, the three automatically priced services and three inquiry-only cards, exact equipment/team counts, SVG bounds, raster-free artwork, option persistence, production price parity, isolated storage and local-only test completion. Review artifacts are saved under `output/ui-reviews/2026-09-28/`; before/after captures of the 2026-10-02 review (1440×900, 1280×700, 1280×660 and 390×844, Korean and English) are under `output/ui-reviews/2026-10-02/`.

Point Playwright at the running dev server with `PLAYWRIGHT_BASE_URL` (port 3000 may belong to another app), and let the server finish recompiling after an edit before starting a run — a run started mid-compile can report a spurious hydration mismatch. Tests that judge movement set `reducedMotion: 'no-preference'`; the config default is `reduce`.

## Archived space, camera and custom-AI options

The previous online estimator included booth construction and area pricing, photo-zone and lighting options, media-art camera upgrades and custom-AI planning, payment integration and maintenance. These options and automatic prices have been removed from the current estimator. The price constants and pure rendering/data helpers remain for other internal references and legacy-data compatibility; they do not contribute to new online totals. The earlier research record remains in [media-art-sensor-pricing.md](media-art-sensor-pricing.md).

Issued estimate links contain a signed snapshot of their original input and calculated result. Reopening or downloading such a link uses that result rather than recalculating with today's inquiry-only rules.
